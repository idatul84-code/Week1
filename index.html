Explanation: Translator vs Interpreter

A closer look at how the two professions differ.

  • Medium: a translator works with books, websites, and documents. An interpreter works with live speech — meetings, conferences, hearings.
  • Time: a translator has time to research and revise. An interpreter must respond instantly, with no time to look anything up.
  • Tools: a translator relies on dictionaries and software. An interpreter relies mainly on memory, quick reflexes, and listening skill.

💡 Key Points

Translating works with written text and allows revision; interpreting works with live speech in real time. Both need bilingual fluency, but interpreting additionally demands memory, composure, and instant recall.

Building Your Interpreting Skills

Interpreting skills develop through deliberate practice. Here are a few techniques you can start using from the very beginning.

🎧 Shadowing Practice

Listen to the speaker and repeat the message aloud, trying to follow the speaker's pace, rhythm and intonation.

💭 Self-Check

  • Did I manage to keep up with the speaker?
  • Where did I fall behind?
  • Which words or phrases were difficult to say?
  • Did I maintain the speaker's rhythm and intonation?

Section 10 of 11

Key Points

💡 Key Points

  • Interpreting works with live spoken language; translation works with written text and allows time to revise.
  • Every interpreter relies on five basic skills: listening, understanding, remembering, reformulating, and delivering.
  • Small details — like a time or a room number — can completely change the message if missed.

Wrap-Up

What you should now be able to do:

  • Explain why interpreting requires more than simply replacing words from one language with another.
  • Identify key skills that support effective interpreting, including listening, memory, reformulation and delivery.
  • Use shadowing to practise keeping pace with a speaker while maintaining rhythm and intonation.
  • Use word chunking to organise a message into meaningful units before rendering it.
  • Paraphrase a message while preserving its original meaning.
